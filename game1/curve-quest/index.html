The error curve you just uncovered

Training error (blue) keeps falling as flexibility rises. Test error (orange) falls, then climbs again. The bottom of the orange curve is the sweet spot. Dashed line is your current degree.

Goal. On each level, choose a model that predicts new data well. You earn up to 3 stars by getting close to the best possible test error.

Rules. 1) The blue dots are your training data. 2) Move the sliders to change the purple curve. 3) Submit to score your model on fresh hidden points. You get a limited number of submits per level, and every submit uses a new hidden sample, so you cannot memorise the test set. 4) Earn at least 1 star to unlock the next level.

Hints. Training error is easy to push to zero. It is not the score. Look at the gap between blue and orange.

After playing, a learner should be able to:

  1. Explain underfitting (high bias): a model too simple to capture the true pattern.
  2. Explain overfitting (high variance): a model that memorises noise, so training error is tiny but test error is large.
  3. Explain why we need a train/test split and why training error is a misleading score.
  4. Show that more data (a bigger sample) reduces variance and allows flexible models.
  5. Use L2 regularisation to control complexity, and describe the bias–variance trade-off.

Under the hood: real least-squares polynomial regression (Chebyshev basis, ridge penalty, solved with Gaussian elimination) on randomly generated noisy data. Nothing is hard-coded.
